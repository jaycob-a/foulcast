# NOTES — Step 11: the public site

Date: 2026-08-11. Branch `step-11`. Follows Step 10's netting join
(`NOTES_STEP10.md`) and the Step 10 update to `AUDIT.md`.

31 park pages plus a home page, static HTML, generated by `site_build.py` from
`site_data.py` and served by `webapp_v2` under `/parks/`.

---

## What this step actually is

Steps 3 through 10 were the model arguing with itself. This one puts the result
in front of somebody who did not read any of it. That changes which of this
project's problems matter. The dominant one is no longer accuracy — it is that
**almost everything worth saying here is unverified, and a page that does not
say so is a lie by layout.**

So the build is organised around four constraints rather than around features,
and each is enforced by `tests/test_site.py` rather than left to care.

### 1. No printed section numbers, anywhere

`AUDIT.md`'s Step 10 update establishes that nineteen of the 31 zone tables in
`stadium.py` are suspect on their numbering: nine contradicted outright by the
club's own current seating map, ten more carrying labels that cannot describe a
continuous seating bowl. There is no way to tell from outside which side of that
line a given park falls on.

A section number is also the single most actionable thing a page like this could
print — it is what a reader would type into a ticket site. So it is the claim
this project can least afford to get wrong, and it is exactly the claim the
audit says is wrong at more than half the fleet.

The site therefore describes seating by position: *the seats at field level
behind home plate*, *the dugout boxes and field seats down the third-base line*,
*the second level on the first-base side*. `site_data.ZONE_WORDS` holds one
phrase per zone ID, and `site_build.area_word()` appends the park's own seating
product word — loge, terrace, grandstand, Diamond Club — with the section range
stripped off. That split is deliberate and follows the audit's revised claim
exactly: the *grouping and deck level* in a zone name is the half still
credited, the *numeric range* is the half condemned, and only the first half
reaches a page.

**What this costs, stated because it is a real loss.** Two clubs publish their
netting heights against section numbers, and the Yankees' page is the most
detailed netting statement in the league. Both had to be re-expressed
positionally, which loses the club's own precision. Both rewrites are in
`site_data.NET_HEIGHT_WORDS` next to the verbatim source string, so the
paraphrase can be checked against what was paraphrased.

Enforcement: three regexes (the word "section" followed by a digit, a printed
range, a prefixed label like `FB17`) plus a per-park check that no raw
`SeatSection.name` and no printed label of three characters or more appears on
that park's page. The three-character floor is a real hole — Fenway, Globe Life
and Truist carry one- and two-character labels that no substring search can tell
from prose — and it is documented in the test module rather than papered over.

### 2. Netting leads; the model follows

Netting comes off a club page. The distribution comes off a simulation that has
never been checked. Putting them in that order on the page is the cheapest
honest thing this site does, and it is asserted per page: the netting section's
anchor must precede the model section's, which must precede the two readings.

### 3. The word "safe" never appears

Not "safe", not "safer", not "safety". `SOURCED_DATA.md` records that almost
every club's own wording is that fans in netted sections *"are still exposed to
objects leaving the field of play"*, and this model has never seen a real foul
ball land. Neither fact supports the word.

The site says **behind netting** / **not behind netting** for what a source
establishes, and **higher risk** / **lower risk** for what the model estimates.
The test is word-bounded so it catches every inflection while leaving
`SafecoField.html` — the real filename of Clem's T-Mobile Park page — alone.

Three drafts of the copy tripped this and all three were reworded rather than
excepted, including a footer that read "should not be used as a safety
guarantee". A disclaimer using the word still teaches the word.

### 4. No accuracy claims

Every park page opens with the statement that nothing on it has been checked
against a real foul ball, and closes with five specific things the model does
not know: that it has never been compared with observed landings, that the bowl
*shape* is unsourced even though its *placement* is not, that a large share of
fouls land where the model has no seats, that the straight-back rate is a guess,
and that left and right are not distinguishable because every park is modelled
as a mirror.

The test bans accuracy vocabulary outright. One casualty was a sentence saying
the site "claims no accuracy figure" — a disclaimer that still trips a keyword
scan, and rewriting it was easier than arguing with the test. One word is
deliberately *not* banned: "precise", because the Royals' own statement, quoted
on the Kauffman page, is that their map cannot show *"the precise location of
the netting"* — which is the opposite of an accuracy claim.

## The two readings

The requirement was both readings off the same data, and the data supports it
directly: `ZoneNetting.status` decides both, and neither is derived from the
other.

- **Catching a ball.** Zones ranked by modelled foul traffic, with anything the
  sources place *fully* behind netting removed, and the removed zones listed
  separately with their traffic intact — "balls still arrive, they just cannot
  be caught". A partly netted zone stays in the list, flagged, because nobody
  publishes where the net's edge falls inside a seating area.
- **What is coming at you.** The same zones, same order, with netting shown
  rather than removed, and the exit velocity attached. The accompanying text
  says the speed is off the bat rather than on arrival, and that a netted zone
  carries the same foul traffic it would without the net.

At a netting-gap park the catching list carries a red panel saying that
**nothing has been excluded**, because nothing could be, and that the top of the
list is where balls arrive rather than where they can be caught. That panel is
the difference between a gap and a blank, and it is asserted per park.

## Netting, as the site presents it

Three states, and a reader has to be able to tell them apart at a glance:

| State | Parks | What the page says |
|---|---:|---|
| Published and joinable | **10** | which areas are behind netting, partly behind it, not behind it, and not mentioned either way — with source URL, source kind, vintage year and retrieval date |
| Published but not joinable | **13** | the club does publish an extent; this model's own seat labels cannot be reconciled with it, and the club is the sourced side of that disagreement. One of the thirteen — Oriole Park — is not a misfit but a mirror: its seating map settles which foul line is which, and the table has them the other way round |
| Not published at all | **8** | which kind of nothing: no text, a club that declines on principle, a page that contradicts itself, or endpoints on a numbering that wraps at an unpublished point |

### Which foul line is which, and why most pages will not say

Printing anything as "the first-base side" is a claim about *which* foul line
is which, and that claim rests on the zone table's printed labels, which
`AUDIT.md`'s revised position holds to be unverified at **every** park and wrong
at nine. The first draft of this step drew that as a hedge: four mapped pages
where the netting came out differently on the two lines carried a warning
telling the reader to take the two lines together.

The seating-map read (below) showed the hedge was too weak. A mirrored table is
not a matter of degree — Oriole Park's two sides are swapped outright, and it
passed every join guard, because every geometry number in `stadium.py` is
mirror-symmetric and so every structural check is blind to a mirror. So the
site now does the stronger thing:

- `seat_map.SIDE_ANCHORS` holds every source in the repo that names a foul-line
  side next to a printed section number — a club page or a direct map read.
  Eleven parks have one; at six the table agrees with it, at one (Oriole Park)
  it is reversed, at two the anchors contradict each other, and at
  twenty-two there is nothing to test against.
- `netting._check_join` gained a fifth guard, G5, that rejects a join when the
  anchored sections all land on the other line. Oriole Park moved from mapped
  to a `sides_flipped` gap. Every mapped park now carries a flag saying whether
  its sides were confirmed (Fenway, Dodger, Truist) or never tested (the other
  seven).
- At the twenty-five parks whose sides are not established, the page folds
  each matching pair of foul-line areas into one row — the mean, not the sum —
  and describes it as both lines at once. No foul line is named there. Only
  the six anchored-and-agreeing parks may name one, and every page states its
  own side position in words. `tests/test_site.py` asserts all three.

The ten gap reasons in `netting.GapKind` are rewritten into plain sentences in
`site_data.GAP_WORDS`, because `ParkJoin.gap_detail` states several of them by
quoting section numbers. The rewrites keep the distinction that matters most —
*nobody published this* versus *somebody published it and this model does not
fit it* — and in the second case the page says outright that the fault is the
model's.

## The sourced figures

Each park page carries its foul territory area and backstop distance with the
basis for the adopted value, the conflicting values where sources disagree, and
what the model does with the number. `tests/test_site.py` asserts that every
figure printed equals the figure `stadium.PARK_PARAMS` actually applies — a page
and a model disagreeing about a number would be the worst failure available
here, so it is checked rather than reviewed.

Three standing caveats ride along, one per figure class, from `PARK_PARAMS.md`:
foul territory is effectively single-sourced (Clem's estimate off his own
diagrams, with Seamheads and FanGraphs both downstream of it); backstop distance
is genuinely contested and the sources disagree because they measure different
things; deck cover is a 2016 figure whose *classification* — whether the cover is
something a foul pop would hit — is a judgment made in `stadium.py` and not a
sourced fact.

Deck cover is included even though the brief named only the first two, because
it is what makes Wrigley's page make sense: 100% of that upper deck is under a
1922 grandstand roof, the model treats that as a blocking structure, and
Wrigley's unmatched share is 52% as a result.

## What is served

`webapp_v2` gained `/parks/` and `/parks/<slug>/`, reading the built HTML from
disk once and caching it in memory. Slugs are matched against the registry
rather than sanitised, so no request-supplied string reaches a filesystem path.
`/robots.txt` and `/sitemap.xml` are generated from the live request host, which
is why the built pages carry no absolute URLs and no canonical tags by default —
pass `--base-url` to emit them for a static deploy.

`site_data.py` is imported defensively: a deployment without the public site
logs a warning and 404s `/parks/`, rather than failing to boot. The Dockerfile
copies `site/` and `site_data.py`, which it would otherwise have missed — its
`COPY` list is explicit.

## What I am least confident about

In order.

**1. The plain-words zone phrases are the weakest thing on the site, and they
are the thing a reader will lean on.** Dropping the section numbers removes a
claim this repo cannot support, but it does not make the replacement true. "The
infield boxes on the first-base side" rests on the same zone table, and the only
part of that table the audit still credits is grouping and deck level — which is
precisely what the phrase asserts, but credited on the strength of a claim that
has itself failed once already. The nine parks whose numbering the clubs
contradict are also the parks whose *grouping* is most likely wrong, and nothing
on the site distinguishes them from the rest on that axis. It flags them as
netting gaps, which is a different thing.

**2. The folded foul-line rows hide a real asymmetry where one exists.** At
the twenty-five parks with no side anchor, the two lines are shown as one area
because the model cannot say which is which. That is honest about the labels
and silent about the park: Clem's sentence about more room on the first-base
side is quoted on Wrigley's page as the one published statement of
foul-territory asymmetry, and the page says the model cannot use it. A folded
row cannot show that asymmetry even in principle. Where the two halves of a
pair disagree on netting, the row says so — one line covered, the other not,
and nothing to say which — and that sentence is the most awkward one on the
site.

**3. The mapped/gap split will read as a quality ranking of ballparks, and it is
not one.** Thirteen of the twenty-one gaps are the model's fault, not the
club's — the club published, this repo's labels do not fit. The pages say so in
words, and the home page now splits the gaps by whose fault they are rather
than listing them as one block. It still reads as a ranking, because "sourced"
against "gap" is a ranking whatever the sub-headings say, and structure wins.

**4. The exit velocity numbers are the least defensible figures on the site.**
They are speed off the bat, and the risk reading is the one place a reader would
most naturally want speed on arrival. The page says which one it is. It says so
in a sentence, next to a list of numbers, and numbers beat sentences.

**5. The souvenir reading tells people where to stand near foul balls.** Every
constraint here pushes toward not overclaiming, and none of them address the
fact that the product's first use is directing a person toward the busiest foul
zone that has no net in front of it. The risk reading is the counterweight and
it is on the same page, at the same size, but a reader who came for one will not
read the other.

**6. Whether static was right.** The pages are fast and indexable and cannot
fail at request time, but they go stale silently: a club moves its netting, and
the page keeps saying what it said in August 2026 until somebody re-runs the
build. Every page carries the retrieval date, which converts a stale claim into
a dated one. It does not refresh it.

## The seating-map read, and what was built on it

`MAP_FINDINGS.md` and the `seating_maps/` directory record a direct read of five
published seating maps — Chase, Fenway, Oriole Park, Sutter Health, Truist —
against the `_make_*_sections()` tables, at 2x-8x around the plate, both foul
lines and the deck edges. Its headline claims are more serious than a numbering
error: the plate zone placed in the wrong part of the park at Chase, Sutter
Health and Truist; the two foul lines **swapped** at Oriole Park; a shift of
several sections at Fenway. Every one of the five disagrees with its table.

The read was not part of the brief, and for the first draft of this step it was
left as an open thread. It is now committed, and the step is built on it in
three places, all described above: the `map_read` family of
`seat_map.SIDE_ANCHORS`, the G5 join guard that used it to knock Oriole Park
out of the mapped list, and the folding of the foul-line rows at every park the
read cannot reach. Each park page shows its own map read where there is one and
says outright that no map has been read where there is not.

The whole `seating_maps/` directory is tracked, including the maps that have
not been read yet — 30 images for 31 parks. They are the evidence the anchors
cite, and the unread ones are the cheapest next correction in the repo: the
plate-zone and boundary findings at the five read parks are recorded in
`MAP_FINDINGS.md` and **not yet applied to the tables**. An anchor that passes
says the table is not mirrored. It says nothing about where the boundaries fall.

## What this does not do

- **No accuracy figure, because none can be computed.** Unchanged from every
  step before this one, and now stated on 32 public pages instead of in a
  markdown file.
- **Nothing physical moved.** No physics or geometry code was touched.
  `site_build.py` calls `predict_game_fouls` exactly as `park_sweep.py` does,
  with the same lineups, seed and pitch mix. The one model-side change is the
  netting join's fifth guard and the side-anchor table behind it, which change
  what the model *claims* about a park and nothing about what it simulates.
- **The numbering defect is not fixed.** It is routed around. `AUDIT.md`'s Step
  10 update still calls correcting the section labels the cheapest large
  correction available in the repo, and it still is — the sources needed have
  already been read. What Step 11 adds is a reason it now blocks something
  visible: 19 of 31 pages describe seating in words because their numbers cannot
  be trusted, and 13 parks show a netting gap that is not a netting gap at all.
