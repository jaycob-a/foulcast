What the club says about netting at Angel Stadium

protective netting is extended between Sections 103-133.

Read on the club's page on 2026-10-20. It said the same when this site first read it, on 2026-08-09.

This page does not say which of its own seating areas that netting stands in front of. Saying so meant reading the club's statement onto this model's seat labels, and this ballpark's own seating map, read under the last heading below, puts this model's areas somewhere other than where their labels say. Find your section in the club's own words instead.

Read the clubs literally: there is "some amount of netting or screening" in front of the listed seats, its "height and coverage will vary by section", and fans behind it "are still exposed to objects leaving the field of play". Behind netting is not fully protected, and no seat here is described as protected outright.

How these figures were produced

One full game, both lineups, the same 18 batters at every park on this site — real hitters chosen to stand for the league, not any one club — so the park is the only thing that changes. 400 simulations per batter, fixed seed, run on 2026-10-21. Every foul the model produces is put in one of four bands by how far along the foul line it comes down: behind the plate up to 50 feet out, near the dugout from 50 to 130 feet, from there to the foul pole, and past the pole. The dugout ends are an estimate from the rule-book layout, and moving them by ten feet either way moves the shares by several points. Every foul counts, whether or not it reaches a seat, because FiveThirtyEight's zones count balls on the field too.

The FiveThirtyEight column is the 906 foul balls FiveThirtyEight logged in 2019, one day at each of 10 parks, each ball placed by eye into one of seven zones, folded here into the same four bands and pooled over all 10 parks. Those days were chosen because they produced unusually many fouls, and a foul-heavy day may not send balls where an ordinary one does. It is set beside every park because it is the only public count there is, not because it is this park's.

About 24% of the fouls this model produces at Angel Stadium land where it has no seating area to put them — deep down the lines near the poles, in the gap in front of the first row, or under a covered deck. They are in the bands above, which count every foul, and they are left out of the seating areas in the next panel, which is why those figures are a shape rather than a census.

Seating areas and decks: untested by any real data

Some areas may be mislabelled. This ballpark's own seating map puts some of them somewhere else; the last panel below says how.

No real data has tested any figure in this panel. The only public count of where foul balls land puts each ball in one of four broad bands, and that is the table above. Which seating area or deck a ball comes down in is finer than anything it records, so these are the model's figures and nothing more: a model estimate, not a count of anything observed, and not good to the tenth they are printed to.

The two foul lines are one area each rather than a first-base area and a third-base one, because nothing says which line is which here. A figure on one of those rows is what reaches one of the two lines, which keeps it comparable with the behind-plate rows beside it. The model builds both lines identically, so the pair would differ only by simulation noise in any case.

The seat labels these areas are built from

Every area on this page is a group of the ballpark's own printed seat labels. Nothing in this model measured them; they were written down from seating charts, and everything above is only as good as they are.

Which foul line is which

Contradicted, and not by a simple reversal. Some of this model's seat labels land on the side their source names and some land on the other, so swapping the two sides would not fix it. The table is wrong in a way that has no single correction, and neither foul line is named here.

What this ballpark's own seating map shows

The Angels' published seating map was read at magnification on 2026-09-07 against the labels this model carries. It is a flat plan in standard orientation, fixed twice over by the legend, which names a left-field pavilion and a right-field pavilion and colours them at opposite ends of the bowl. As a source it is good. It disagrees with this model in the following ways.

Contradicted: what this page would call the third-base baseline is the far end of the opposite foul line. The map puts the first three positions of that area at the outfield end of the right-field arm — not near the plate, and not on third base. The rest of that area names seats that are not in the building at field level at all. Fourteen anchored labels agree with the map and three disagree, so this is not a mirror and a swap would not fix it. No area on this page is called first-base or third-base.

The area this page calls the seats behind home plate is about six positions up the third-base line. What this model calls the first-base infield is the plate block itself — the map names it as a single club arc — and part of what it calls the first-base baseline is behind the plate too.

Two published runs from this club, disagreeing on length, agree exactly on where the plate is. The map prints a netting extent in words, and the club's netting page publishes a longer one. They are centred on the same position, and that position is the middle of the plate block the map draws. Two independent statements from the same club, both putting the plate where this model does not.

The club's published netting run, on this model's numbering, would begin and end on the same foul line. It would start in a third-base area, cross the plate and both first-base areas, and finish inside another third-base area — a run that passes through one foul line to get from one end of the other to itself. On the map's numbering it is one continuous stretch through the plate. That is why this model could not attach the club's netting to its own areas, stated as geometry.

Two areas on the upper rings are at the wrong end, or not in the building. The area this model calls the third-base club level begins at the first-base end of that ring, and the area it calls the third-base upper deck begins at the first-base end of that one and then runs past where the ring stops.

None of this has been corrected in the model. Correcting it means rebuilding this park's seating table against the map, which is a change to the model and not to this page, and it has not been done.
