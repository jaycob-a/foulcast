What the club says about netting at Rogers Centre

The protective netting extends down the length of the first and third baseline walls, to Sections 113C & 130C, respectively, tapering off to the curve before the foul poles. The height of the extended netting matches what was in place behind home plate previously (30 feet), and the colour and knotting is the same as well.

Read on the club's page on 2026-10-20. It said the same when this site first read it, on 2026-08-09.

This page does not say which of its own seating areas that netting stands in front of. Saying so meant reading the club's statement onto this model's seat labels, and this ballpark's own seating map, read under the last heading below, puts this model's areas somewhere other than where their labels say. Find your section in the club's own words instead.

Read the clubs literally: there is "some amount of netting or screening" in front of the listed seats, its "height and coverage will vary by section", and fans behind it "are still exposed to objects leaving the field of play". Behind netting is not fully protected, and no seat here is described as protected outright.

How these figures were produced

One full game, both lineups, the same 18 batters at every park on this site — real hitters chosen to stand for the league, not any one club — so the park is the only thing that changes. 400 simulations per batter, fixed seed, run on 2026-10-21. Every foul the model produces is put in one of four bands by how far along the foul line it comes down: behind the plate up to 50 feet out, near the dugout from 50 to 130 feet, from there to the foul pole, and past the pole. The dugout ends are an estimate from the rule-book layout, and moving them by ten feet either way moves the shares by several points. Every foul counts, whether or not it reaches a seat, because FiveThirtyEight's zones count balls on the field too.

The FiveThirtyEight column is the 906 foul balls FiveThirtyEight logged in 2019, one day at each of 10 parks, each ball placed by eye into one of seven zones, folded here into the same four bands and pooled over all 10 parks. Those days were chosen because they produced unusually many fouls, and a foul-heavy day may not send balls where an ordinary one does. It is set beside every park because it is the only public count there is, not because it is this park's.

About 18% of the fouls this model produces at Rogers Centre land where it has no seating area to put them — deep down the lines near the poles, in the gap in front of the first row, or under a covered deck. They are in the bands above, which count every foul, and they are left out of the seating areas in the next panel, which is why those figures are a shape rather than a census.

Seating areas and decks: untested by any real data

Some areas may be mislabelled. This ballpark's own seating map puts some of them somewhere else; the last panel below says how.

No real data has tested any figure in this panel. The only public count of where foul balls land puts each ball in one of four broad bands, and that is the table above. Which seating area or deck a ball comes down in is finer than anything it records, so these are the model's figures and nothing more: a model estimate, not a count of anything observed, and not good to the tenth they are printed to.

The seat labels these areas are built from

Every area on this page is a group of the ballpark's own printed seat labels. Nothing in this model measured them; they were written down from seating charts, and everything above is only as good as they are.

Which foul line is which

Established, against a source that names a side. A source outside this model says which foul line is which — the club naming a side beside specific seats, or the park's own seating map read with a landmark fixing which way round the drawing runs — and every seat label this model puts on one line lands on that line in the source. That is why this page names the two sides. It establishes nothing else: not where the boundaries between areas fall, not whether the areas exist.

What this ballpark's own seating map shows

The Blue Jays' published seating map was read at magnification on 2026-09-07 against the labels this model carries. It is a flat plan in standard orientation that draws the diamond explicitly, with the bases marked — but what fixed it is the club's own netting page, which has named a side alongside a specific seat on each foul line since long before any map was read, and the map agrees with both. As a source it is good. It disagrees with this model in the following ways.

The sides were already established here, and the map extends the evidence from two positions to twenty-six. This is the one ballpark of its round that did not need a map to name its foul lines. What the map adds is breadth: instead of one anchored seat on each line it now supplies a long run on each. It also draws the home dugout on the third-base arm — another map that would have mirrored the park if a dugout label had been trusted.

The area this page calls the seats behind home plate is about thirteen positions up the first-base line. The map runs one closed ring with the plate in the middle of it; this model has the plate at the low end with both foul lines running the same way from it. The behind-plate figure is the largest on this page and it is attached to seats on a foul line.

The two upper rings are about seven positions the same way. Both behind-plate areas sit on the first-base side of the block the map has behind the plate on their own ring, and on all three rings the first-base baseline area straddles the plate and runs on into the third-base seats.

This model claims the same seat label on both foul lines at once. One printed label at field level appears in both a first-base area and a third-base area of this model's table. It is resolved to the first-base side by a tie-break rule rather than by anything about the ballpark, which is worth knowing when reading where one area ends and the next begins.

The map draws no netting, so this park's netting position is unchanged by it. There is no netting entry in the legend and no line along the field edge. A pale arc does run behind one stretch of seats, but it is on the concourse side and carries an accessible-seating icon, so it is a platform edge. A drawing that shows no netting is not evidence that a park has none.

None of this has been corrected in the model. Correcting it means rebuilding this park's seating table against the map, which is a change to the model and not to this page, and it has not been done.
