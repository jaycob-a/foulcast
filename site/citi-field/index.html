What the club says about netting at Citi Field

Read on the club's seat map on 2026-10-20.

The club's seat map carries no netting text. The club's netting page, which carried none either when it was read on 2026-08-09, no longer exists: it answered not-found on 2026-10-20. A second-hand figure for this park exists, from a ticketing site, and has never been checked against anything the club publishes, so it is not used here.

Read the clubs literally: there is "some amount of netting or screening" in front of the listed seats, its "height and coverage will vary by section", and fans behind it "are still exposed to objects leaving the field of play". Behind netting is not fully protected, and no seat here is described as protected outright.

How these figures were produced

One full game, both lineups, the same 18 batters at every park on this site — real hitters chosen to stand for the league, not any one club — so the park is the only thing that changes. 400 simulations per batter, fixed seed, run on 2026-10-21. Every foul the model produces is put in one of four bands by how far along the foul line it comes down: behind the plate up to 50 feet out, near the dugout from 50 to 130 feet, from there to the foul pole, and past the pole. The dugout ends are an estimate from the rule-book layout, and moving them by ten feet either way moves the shares by several points. Every foul counts, whether or not it reaches a seat, because FiveThirtyEight's zones count balls on the field too.

The FiveThirtyEight column is the 906 foul balls FiveThirtyEight logged in 2019, one day at each of 10 parks, each ball placed by eye into one of seven zones, folded here into the same four bands and pooled over all 10 parks. Those days were chosen because they produced unusually many fouls, and a foul-heavy day may not send balls where an ordinary one does. It is set beside every park because it is the only public count there is, not because it is this park's.

About 25% of the fouls this model produces at Citi Field land where it has no seating area to put them — deep down the lines near the poles, in the gap in front of the first row, or under a covered deck. They are in the bands above, which count every foul, and they are left out of the seating areas in the next panel, which is why those figures are a shape rather than a census.

Seating areas and decks: untested by any real data

Some areas may be mislabelled. This ballpark's own seating map puts some of them somewhere else; the last panel below says how.

No real data has tested any figure in this panel. The only public count of where foul balls land puts each ball in one of four broad bands, and that is the table above. Which seating area or deck a ball comes down in is finer than anything it records, so these are the model's figures and nothing more: a model estimate, not a count of anything observed, and not good to the tenth they are printed to.

The seat labels these areas are built from

Every area on this page is a group of the ballpark's own printed seat labels. Nothing in this model measured them; they were written down from seating charts, and everything above is only as good as they are.

Which foul line is which

Established, against a source that names a side. A source outside this model says which foul line is which — the club naming a side beside specific seats, or the park's own seating map read with a landmark fixing which way round the drawing runs — and every seat label this model puts on one line lands on that line in the source. That is why this page names the two sides. It establishes nothing else: not where the boundaries between areas fall, not whether the areas exist.

What this ballpark's own seating map shows

The Mets' published seating map was read at magnification on 2026-09-07 against the labels this model carries. It is a flat plan in standard orientation that names no base and no field anywhere on the sheet — every word on it was magnified and read. Which way round it runs was settled by measuring the drawn outfield against this park's published dimensions: the drawn wall is deeper on one side at every angle from twenty degrees out, matching a ballpark whose right-centre is forty feet deeper than its left-centre, and a mirrored drawing misses that fit by about forty feet on both sides at once. As a source it is excellent — every number resolved at three to eight times magnification. It disagrees with this model in the following ways.

The mildest disagreement of the thirty maps read, and the only ballpark whose behind-plate area at field level is already centred on the plate. The map puts the plate on the boundary between the two middle positions of this model's behind-plate area, with an equal number of positions each side. No other park on this site can say that. Worth stating plainly given what the twenty-nine other reads found.

The seat labels run the right way round on all three rings this model numbers. Forty-six anchored labels agree with the map and none disagree, across field level and both upper rings. That is why this page names its foul lines.

One ring up the behind-plate area is about two positions toward third base, and two rings up it is about five. On the top ring every one of that area's nine positions is on the third-base side of the plate. The drift is in the same direction on both rings, and there is a reason for it in the building: the rings at this ballpark are not concentric with each other, so a table that divides each of them into three roughly equal arcs will drift the same way on every ring it does not measure.

This model is silent about most of this ballpark. The map prints two hundred and twenty-five seat labels across five numbered rings and three premium rings behind the plate. This model names about a third of them and says nothing at all about two whole rings. The figures are a shape across the part of the ballpark this model carries, not a census of it.

A netting compilation this site holds for this park is symmetric about the same plate boundary the map draws. It was arrived at by an unrelated route and it agrees, which is worth recording. It says nothing about which foul line is which, and it does not change the statement above: this club publishes nothing about where its netting runs, and the compilation is not the club.

None of this has been corrected in the model. Correcting it means rebuilding this park's seating table against the map, which is a change to the model and not to this page, and it has not been done.
